ABSTRACT 

Expressive speech systems make a decision before any waveform is rendered: how an utterance is delivered. In dialogue agents, narration, and role-conditioned TTS, that hidden planning step sets affect, pitch, energy, rate, pause, emphasis, and stance, yet downstream audio scores rarely reveal whether those choices were li-censed by the source record, a source-use failure that occurs before any waveform exists. VoxReason makes that pre-synthesis decision measurable as a listener-free task for source-grounded speech planning. Before synthesis, VoxReason measures whether delivery choices are grounded in cited source records. Systems output a source-cited speaking-plan with evidence citations, and a deterministic verifier checks citation legality, slot agreement, unsupported state, schema validity, and one-cue counterfactual locality. On 1,440 checked source-label cases, shortcut controls show why slot accuracy alone is unsafe: a key-lookup oracle reaches 1.000 plan-slot accuracy on seen keys, while an emotion prior still reaches 0.958 slot accuracy on source-key-disjoint cases without citing intensity or identity. In a separate 100-case learned source-key-disjoint comparison, a 7B locality SFT+CF repair improves plan-slot accuracy/locality from 0.684/0.141 to 0.919/1.000, and removing source records lowers citation-required grounded score by 0.488. Ren-dered waveform quality remains outside the present evaluation. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Expressive speech systems increasingly determine utterance delivery as part of generation itself. In dialogue agents, narration systems, and role-conditioned TTS, the same sentence may require relief, warning, impatience, or reassurance depending on the source record that defines the scene. That delivery decision is usually made before synthesis, then hidden inside the final waveform. Once the waveform is scored, it is difficult to tell whether the system actually used the record that licensed the delivery or simply produced a plausible voice from a memorized script. A model may therefore sound correct for the wrong reason. What is missing is the accountable decision between context understanding and synthesis. VoxRea-son exposes that hidden decision as a source-cited speaking plan: a structured prediction in which cited records justify affect, pitch, energy, rate, pause, emphasis, and stance. Delivery therefore be-comes a checkable prediction checked against the records a system cites, rather than an implicit style choice buried in the final waveform. The schema admits speech, dialogue, role, and scene citations. In the evaluated source-label setting, role and scene fields remain fixed so the citation channel is tested directly. A valid planner identifies the decisive cue, chooses only the delivery fields supported by that cue, avoids invented state, and revises only the affected fields when a single cue changes. Every accepted field therefore carries a record-level justification before any waveform is generated. The resulting challenge is not to sound persuasive, but to show genuine record dependence. Aplanner may still look competent by mapping an emotion word to a memorized social script and then producing a fluent explanation for it. That appearance breaks when the source record changes while the utterance text stays fixed. VoxReason therefore treats ordinary slot accuracy as necessary but insufficient. The stronger test asks whether performance survives unseen source keys and source emotions without collapsing into a label prior. The source-label testbed isolates this failure mode. We cast source-grounded speech planning as a pre-synthesis prediction problem: given a source record, the model must produce delivery fields justified by explicit citations. We start from a narrow source-label setting because it isolates the failure mode of interest: when the utterance stays fixed and the licensing cue changes, does the plan change with it? VoxReason combines a listener-free verifier, a controlled source-label testbed, and matched 7B comparisons in which removing source records lowers citation-required grounded score by 0.488 and plan-slot accuracy by 0.599. The public GitHub repository 1 provides the derived split and evaluation code for this evaluated setting (Figure 1). 

2 RELATED WORK 

Recent context-aware TTS and speech role-playing systems show that delivery depends on context rather than transcript alone Chen et al. (2026); Xue et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2025). Speech-reasoning benchmarks make the same point with timestamped evidence, prosody-conditioned actions, transcript-acoustic conflicts, affect explanations, auditory intelligence, and audio rout-ing Jeong et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2026b); Lu et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2026); Jin et al. (2026); Ma et al. (2026); Zhang et al. (2026). Multimodal generation, spatial audio, video-audio synthesis, and representation studies further show that sound-related reasoning depends on what information survives into downstream decisions Liu et al. (2025; 2026a); Tian et al. (2026); Sun et al. (2025); Ghosh et al. (2026); Ye et al. (2026); Kang et al. (2025); Ye et al. (2024). What these settings rarely expose, however, is the accountable pre-synthesis decision itself: which record authorized a delivery field, and whether that record was actually used. Process-reward work treats intermediate reasoning traces as measurable objects Chan et al. (2026), while broad audio-reasoning systems and reward-model evaluations usually score final answers, waveforms, or rated outcomes Ma et al. (2025; 2026); Tu et al. (2026); Jin et al. (2025). VoxReason inserts a measurement layer before that stage. A delivery field receives credit only when the model names an allowed source record, matches that record, and reacts locally when the licensed cue

3 A T ESTBED FOR SHORTCUT FALSIFICATION 

VoxReason begins with a controlled source-label testbed because the paper’s core question is causal, not stylistic: when the same utterance is paired with a different licensing cue, does the delivery plan change for the right reason? Each example contains dialogue context, optional context-audio fields, a role profile, target text, evidence cues, a gold speaking plan, and counterfactual cue edits. Cue fields cover lexical content, pitch, energy, rhythm, pause, overlap, speaker role, scene state, dialogue act, and emotion. The intervention holds the words and most context fields fixed while changing the cited source label, forcing the planner to reveal whether it follows the record or a memorized delivery template. This narrowness is the experimental intervention: by reducing text, role, and scene variation, the test concentrates power on the link from source record to delivery field. Appendix Table 3 gives trace-level examples of how the same case either licenses a delivery-field change or rejects a shortcut that lacks the required source citation. The point of this construction is not benchmark breadth; it is to make source-use failure directly observable under a controlled intervention. 

Data construction. Two data splits serve different purposes. The deterministic 108-case boot-strap split checks the schema and evaluator but is not used for empirical claims. The source-label study uses 1,440 RAVDESS records Livingstone & Russo (2018) after source parsing, metadata mapping, plan reconstruction, cue checks, counterfactual labels, confidence policy, license fields, leakage checks, and split integrity all pass; the learned full100 comparisons keep the earlier fixed 100-case protocol for model-to-model comparability. An additional listener-free import check uses 1,800 CREMA-D clips Cao et al. (2014) to test the same source-label criteria across 12 statement labels and 23 actors; because scene/context labels remain limited, this cross-corpus check tight-ens the limitation rather than broadening the main benchmark claim. A larger benchmark would add licensed speech/audio indexes, independent cue and plan annotations, pre-adjudication agree-ment, adjudicated gold labels, and public files that exclude private audio, credentials, checkpoints, and rater identifiers. This paper uses no human listening study; such extensions require separate licensed audio ingestion and adjudication. The present route is diagnostic by design rather than benchmark-complete. Public context audio is absent, only two target utterance texts appear, scene labels are held fixed, and each observed source emotion-intensity key maps to one gold plan. Those restrictions block broad text-, scene-, and audio-generalization claims. They also sharpen the falsification test: if a learned planner still improves under leave-key-out keys, prior-only controls, and one-cue counterfactual edits, the gain is harder to explain away as a direct key-to-plan lookup. 

Annotation reliability. Reliability is handled differently across the two routes. For newly anno-tated data, reliability is measured before adjudication with cue-type, cue-label, and full-cue Jaccard agreement; macro and per-slot Cohen’s kappa for speaking-plan labels; and conflict rates that re-quire adjudication. The source-label route instead uses deterministic reconstruction from licensed source records. Annotator disagreement belongs to the expansion stage. The two data routes carry different uncertainty. Table 15 separates deterministic reconstruction, pre-adjudication disagreement, adjudicated labels, and later audio endpoints, so the paper only makes claims from source-label cases whose admissibility is fixed before evaluation. The core ambiguity is local and inspectable. In Table 2, the lexical sentence is fixed, but the source emotion cue changes the speaking plan. The neutral counterfactual specifies which slots move and which slots stay fixed. A rationale fails when it misses the source cue or changes fields that the edit did not touch, even if the prose sounds plausible. The benchmark stressors separate what is measured now from what later expansion must add. In the reported source-label setting, difficulty comes from holding words fixed while changing licensed labels; broader role, scene, and acoustic variation remain future evaluation requirements rather than 3generalization claims. This design makes the shortcut visible: text that sounds reasonable still fails when its delivery choices are not attached to the record that made them valid. The minimal-pair case is the unit of evaluation. A planner earns credit only when it cites the source label that licenses the delivery fields and, after a neutral edit, changes only the linked slots. The case is small by design, so the failure remains inspectable without a listening panel. Table 2 gives one fixed-utterance locality example, while Figure 2 documents the full label and split coverage of the dataset. The source labels are also checked against waveform anchors from RAVDESS. Strong-intensity records have higher RMS than normal-intensity records, and the plan-linked pitch, energy, and rate labels track rough pitch, RMS, and zero-crossing contrasts in the expected direction. These an-chors support source-label consistency; they do not substitute for broader role, scene, or waveform evidence. The separate bootstrap split remains a calibration set and is not used as empirical benchmark scale. 

4 METHOD 

VoxReason is built around one design choice: make the sentence-to-delivery decision a first-class prediction before a renderer hides it inside audio. Its method has three layers: a source-cited speak-ing plan as the prediction object, a deterministic verifier as the admissibility filter, and a fixed endpoint hierarchy for comparing verifier-clean outputs. The planner receives transcript context, licensed acoustic features when available, role metadata, and the target utterance, then returns con-strained JSON with evidence citations and speaking-plan slots for emotion, intent, pitch, energy, rate, pauses, emphasis, and stance. 

Input modalities and controls. Speech features enter as evidence fields, not as a separate scor-ing target. The no-listener track keeps transcript-only, dependency-free acoustic-statistics, frozen speech-encoder, optional codec-token, and shuffled-cue controls on shared case IDs and decoding settings; broader representation claims require fixed tasks, fixed decoders, complete coverage, and strict-schema outputs. 

Task definition. A speech reasoning instance is xi = ( ci, a i, r i, u i), where ci is dialogue/context text, ai is optional licensed context audio, ri is role or speaker context, and ui is the target utterance. The gold process annotation contains an evidence set Ei ⊂ E and a structured speaking plan yi ∈Y. Systems return cited evidence ˆEi and a plan ˆyi; a renderer, if used, is downstream of this prediction. A prediction is faithful when ˆEi matches annotated cues, ˆyi matches the gold plan, and a counterfactual cue edit changes only the expected plan slots. The training interface used by learned planners combines supervised plan likelihood with process rewards, 

L(θ) = Lsft (θ) − λE RE ( ˆE, E ) − λY RY (ˆ y, y ) − λC RC (ˆ y, ˆycf ), (1) where RE rewards evidence precision/recall, RY rewards slot-level plan agreement, and RC rewards local counterfactual consistency. This expression defines the scoring objective and reportable end-points; the learned configurations instantiate it through supervised schema-aligned plan likelihood and, for the preference configurations, pairwise DPO updates summarized in Table 31. The reported automatic metrics are evidence F1, plan-slot accuracy, hallucinated evidence rate, grounded score, citation-required grounded score, and counterfactual consistency. Evidence legality and slot validity are admissibility conditions; grounded score and citation-required grounded score summarize pre-dictions that pass those conditions; counterfactual consistency then tests whether a cited cue change produces the right local plan change rather than a broad rewrite. Rendering-side claims require separate backend provenance and downstream study. 

Falsifiable claim. VoxReason tests a single property: a speech planner changes delivery fields only when cited source cues license those changes. A system that succeeds by memorizing source-emotion/intensity keys, copying lexical priors, or moving unrelated slots after a one-cue edit fails the claim even if its explanation is fluent. Source-disjoint splits and counterfactual locality checks are therefore preconditions for evidence-use claims, not optional robustness extras. 

Verifier and metrics. Passing the verifier establishes four record-level facts: every cited source exists in the case, every scored slot matches the label licensed by that source, unsupported sources 4are absent, and a one-cue edit changes only the slots tied to the edited cue. These facts are sufficient for the record-level process checks reported here and insufficient for generated-waveform endpoints, so the no-listener scores are not used for downstream audio claims. Errors are counted at the verifier interface: source legality, slot agreement, cue locality, and coverage. The metrics inherit this verifier definition. Evidence F1 scores cited records; plan-slot accuracy scores delivery fields; grounded score combines the two while penalizing unsupported sources; and the citation-required variant zeros the scalar whenever required gold evidence is missing. Fixed weights are only a reporting convention, so final comparisons must keep the same direction under alternative weights and complete seed-case coverage. The listener-free endpoint orders verifier-clean plans; it does not rescue plans that fail source support or serve as an audio-preference proxy. The scalar reflects citation and slot licensing, not perceived audio quality. Its weights balance licensed-source retrieval against licensed plan-slot assignment, with a smaller penalty for unsup-ported citations because that error is already visible in evidence precision. The source-label gold plan is a deterministic reference induced by the released source-label mapping rather than a claim that speech delivery has a single universally correct rendering. Cases with multiple defensible de-liveries enter the benchmark only after the uncertainty and adjudication policy in Table 15. 

5 EXPERIMENTS 

The experiments follow a falsification ladder. If expressive speech planning is genuinely evidence-grounded, adding the source record must change the cited plan, removing the record must hurt, and shortcut baselines must fail once memorized keys no longer apply. The primary comparison therefore asks whether a structured planner ties delivery decisions to supplied evidence better than transcript-only and free-form-rationale baselines. Representation probes test whether plan-relevant cues survive encoding, one-cue edits test local slot movement, and ASR/noise perturbations test whether citations break before surface fluency breaks. Every endpoint below is computed from the case record and planner JSON; rendered waveforms remain outside the reported endpoints unless a separate evaluation protocol covers them. We separate primary comparisons from diagnostic ones before reading any table. Primary com-parisons require complete expected predictions, strict schema validity, recorded provenance, and a listener-free endpoint fixed before inspection. Diagnostic comparisons probe a parser, lookup ceiling, acoustic anchor, or partial learned run; they expose failure modes but do not rank mod-els. Under this rule, the source-key and source-emotion settings are controlled source-disjoint stress tests, whereas the full100 source-label comparison is the complete comparison used for the central record-dependence result. Comparisons with incomplete seed coverage or listener-only endpoints remain outside the reported model comparison. For each prediction, the verifier follows citations back to the case record. It checks that cited cues exist, cue-linked slots match the source labels, unsupported scene or speaker state is absent, and the counterfactual edit changes the intended slot while leaving unrelated slots stable. A coherent explanation still fails when the cited record does not license the plan. The ablations ask what evidence channel the planner used. Each contrast in Table 19 is paired with the failure mode it is meant to expose and the readout that carries that interpretation. 

Structured-output stress test. The PromptClean counterfactual stress test evaluates repeated gener-ation under the JSON schema. All ten configured shards have been collected, giving 960 seed-mode outputs with strict-schema-clean JSON and zero schema, parse, or cue-verification errors (Table 20). The run stresses the parser and cue verifier at the scale used by later planner runs; model ranking still follows the seed-complete ablation protocol. The ten-shard PromptClean contrast uses one parser and one cue-verification path across all modes. Counterfactual consistency multiplies expected-change accuracy by one minus the unexpected-change rate, so it rewards moving the edited slot while leaving unrelated slots fixed. Evidence-grounded outputs score 0.1748, above the shuffled-cue control at 0.1643 with paired improvement 0.0105 and 95% CI [0.0090, 0.0120]. Text-evidence control remains higher at 0.1798, with a paired difference of −0.0050 and 95% CI [ −0.0083 ,−0.0017 ] for evidence-grounded prompting. This pat-5tern supports sensitivity to cue format and the parser path, not superiority of record-level citation over equivalent prose evidence, so Table 21 and Figure 4 are reported as calibration results. Output validity is filtered before score comparison: the initial schema+DPO setting fails on 25 of 54 tracked outputs, mostly through unparseable JSON, while the lower-learning-rate repair and schema-aligned runs are strictly valid on their tracked outputs. The corresponding training objective, reused initialization, and planned change in Table 31 tie the SFT and preference-tuning comparisons to reproducible recipes rather than unnamed prompt variants. These configurations test the structured-output interface and are excluded from final learned-model ordering evidence. 

Shortcut baselines and ceiling checks. The 1,440 source-label cases pass deterministic recon-struction checks before they enter any source-label analysis: source parsing, metadata mapping, gold-plan reconstruction, gold-cue checks, counterfactual-label checks, confidence policy, license fields, and split coverage. The label coverage and upper-bound contrast in Table 9 sets the inter-pretation for later learned results. In that contrast, copying the source labels gives 1.000 evidence F1, 1.000 decisive-cue recall, 1.000 plan-slot accuracy, and 1.000 citation-required grounded score, while the text-neutral control reaches 0.857, 0.000, 0.183, and 0.426 and leaves 0.250 of required cues uncited. Decisive-cue recall measures whether a system cites the cue changed by the local counterfactual edit, so the contrast exposes source use directly rather than relying only on aggre-gate evidence F1. This contrast establishes the metric ceiling and provenance path before learned generalization is claimed. The construct-validity check makes the shortcut explicit: the public source-label route has zero pub-lic context-audio records, two target utterances, 24 role labels, one scene label, and fifteen emotion-intensity keys (Table 6). The table runs from direct measurement coverage to shortcut pressure, making clear in one place what is measured, what remains absent, and why the claim stops at a controlled source-label diagnostic. All 1,440 gold plans use labels admitted by the planner schema, every observed key maps to a single gold plan, and the one-cue counterfactual edits record the slot-wise delta induced by the edited emotion label. These facts make the experiment a falsification instrument rather than a leaderboard: a successful planner must beat label shortcuts by citing the changed record and moving the licensed fields under held-out source keys. A deterministic train-set lookup keyed only by source emotion and intensity reaches 1.000 exact-plan and plan-slot accuracy when test keys are seen, but drops to 0.000 exact-plan accuracy and 0.242 slot accuracy when same-key training cases are removed. An emotion-only prior still reaches 0.953 plan-slot accuracy on the original split, showing why slot accuracy alone is unsafe. Reparti-tioning the same 100 cases into a source-key-disjoint split gives 60 train, 16 development, and 24 test cases with zero source-key overlap; the disjoint emotion-only prior still reaches 0.958 plan-slot accu-racy without citations or source intensity (Table 1). We therefore use this split as a controlled stress test for locality and citation use, not as benchmark-scale evidence on its own. Two strict-schema-clean source-key configurations have complete original-case and edited-case coverage: the 7B con-figuration reaches 1.000 evidence F1, 0.684 plan-slot accuracy, 0.858 citation-required grounded score, and 0.141 counterfactual consistency, while the JSON-normalized 3B repair reaches 1.000, 0.546, 0.796, and 0.148. The pre-specified 7B locality SFT+CF configuration reaches 1.000 evi-dence F1, 0.919 plan-slot accuracy, 0.964 citation-required grounded score, and 1.000 counterfac-tual consistency. Table 8 summarizes the repair gains over the 7B source-key configuration: +0.236 plan-slot accuracy, +0.859 counterfactual consistency, +0.106 citation-required grounded score, and +0.238 weakest-key citation-required score. The original 3B source-key run remains excluded be-cause of schema failures, and the source-emotion prior remains a shortcut warning because its high plan-slot score has no citation channel. The stricter source-emotion split holds out affect labels themselves: ‘angry’ and ‘calm’ are test-only, ‘fearful’ is development-only, and the emotion-only prior drops to 0.000 exact-plan accuracy and 0.219 plan-slot accuracy. Two schema-clean learned configurations in Table 5 have complete seed coverage: the 3B JSON-normalization configuration reaches 0.616 plan-slot accuracy and 0.445 counterfactual consistency, while the 7B configuration reaches 0.621 plan-slot accuracy and 1.000 counterfactual consistency. Statistical protocol. Automatic comparisons use paired per-case bootstrap intervals when systems share examples; seed-level tables summarize repeated runs over the fixed source-label test split. In tables and figures, ± denotes standard deviation across seeds unless a paired confidence interval is named, and lower-is-better improvements are sign-flipped so positive values favor the candidate. An ablation comparison enters the analysis only after complete prediction coverage and strict schema-valid outputs. The primary endpoint is paired change in citation-required grounded score, followed by evidence F1, plan-slot accuracy, ungated grounded score, and hallucinated-evidence rate under Holm ordering. No planned seed is silently dropped; incomplete families remain provisional, and decoding repairs use new dated candidate names. 

Bootstrap calibration. The deterministic bootstrap split calibrates data construction and verifier execution: 108 cases, 324 gold cues, and 108 counterfactual edits have zero validation errors (Ta-ble 24). On that split, the rule baseline reaches 0.5722 evidence F1, 0.2975 plan-slot accuracy, and 0.4914 grounded score, and the scenario-disjoint lexical retrieval check provides a stricter reference point for failure analysis (Tables 25 and 26). Because the bootstrap cases are template-generated and not yet manually reviewed with licensed audio, these values test evaluator behavior rather than empirical model behavior. Every scenario-disjoint retrieval case fails at least one strict grounding threshold. The failures in Table 27 concentrate in missed scene cues, unsupported transferred cues, and wrong intent or de-livery fields. These slices define the stress test for learned planners; the pilot split remains outside empirical claims. Case-level retrieval errors follow the same pattern. The examples in Table 28 show how the re-trieval baseline borrows cues from lexically nearby training cases; the verifier records which source cues were missed, which cues were transferred without support, and which plan slots changed as a consequence. 6 RESULTS : S OURCE RECORDS CHANGE PLANS 

The main result tests record dependence with the words held fixed. VoxReason asks whether the planner changes for the right reason when the source record changes. This is a dependence result, not a leaderboard. The evidence comes in three linked pieces: a 1,440-case source-label route that is fully checked rather than anecdotal; matched 7B comparisons in which source access changes grounded planning more than model scale alone; and shortcut controls showing why that gain mat-ters, because lookup and prior baselines recover many slots without lawful citations whereas the locality-repaired planner retains both citation grounding and edited-cue locality under the harder holdouts. Throughout the paper, those claims are restricted to listener-free endpoints whose admis-sibility is fixed before inspection. The core result is judged on citation-required grounded score because the claimed gain has to sur-vive both failure modes at once: the plan must choose the right slots and cite the source records that license them. Before any comparison is read, the endpoint, pairing unit, bootstrap rule, ordered 7secondary measures, controls, and seed-completion rule are fixed. Citation identifiers are not deco-ration: deleting, swapping, or inventing them from an otherwise correct plan causes rejection. As a result, the ungated score remains secondary so omitted required citations do not receive credit from a low unsupported-source rate. The source-label claim is reproducible rather than anecdotal: all 1,440 cases pass source-record, metadata, gold-plan, gold-cue, counterfactual, confidence, and license checks, with coverage across dialogue context, role-scene fields, acoustic-prosody tags, temporal-audio tags, and counterfactual edits. Source access changes grounded planning more than model scale alone. With the same 7B family and decoding setup, the source-labelled planner exceeds the transcript-only configuration by 0.488 citation-required grounded score and 0.599 plan-slot accuracy, while reducing unsupported evidence by 0.139; the matching model-scale contrast on the full100 setting is only 0.023 citation-required grounded score and 0.051 plan-slot accuracy (Appendix Table 12). The separation is stable under final-interval resampling, robustness checks, and alternative metric weights, and the source-channel lower confidence bounds exceed the model-scale upper bounds on citation-required and plan-slot endpoints. The result answers the paper’s central question directly: in this controlled setting, access to the licensing record changes the planned delivery fields in a way that survives stricter checks than slot accuracy alone. The typed citation format is therefore better viewed as an auditing interface than as a settled prompt-ing recipe: Appendix Table 21 shows that an equivalent text-evidence control is competitive with, and in one aggregate contrast slightly exceeds, the typed format. What VoxReason fixes is the verifier: it lets those alternatives be tested under shared cue-shuffling, transcript-only, prior, and local-counterfactual controls. Shortcut baselines show why the gain matters rather than merely that it exists. A lookup keyed by source emotion and intensity saturates seen keys, while an emotion-only prior recovers many plan slots without record citations (Table 6). Under leave-key-out evaluation, the lookup falls from 1.000 exact accuracy to 0.000 exact accuracy and 0.242 slot accuracy; the prior still reaches 0.953 slot ac-curacy. These controls matter because memorization is easy, slot accuracy overstates source use, and locality is the missing ingredient. Counterfactual locality is decisive: when an edited source cue con-tradicts the prior, a grounded planner must cite the edited record and change only the slots licensed by that edit. For that reason, we treat the source-label route as an intentionally narrow stress test, not as a broad benchmark. The learned-planner claim enters only after the direct key-to-plan shortcut fails under leave-key-out keys, the affect prior remains visible, and the edited-cue locality check still passes. In the full100 comparison, the hardest residual emotions are disgust and surprised, but the learned planner still gains +0.439 and +0.496 citation-required grounded score over the transcript-only route in those slices, and the dominant remaining failures are rate- and stance-slot mismatches rather than missed evidence. The remaining gap is therefore one of delivery-field calibration after cue retrieval, not cue omission itself. Completed learned configurations support source-grounding in the controlled source-label holdouts, while broader benchmark and model-ranking conclusions still require comparable ablation evidence on richer splits. 

7 LIMITATIONS AND ETHICS 

The controlled source-label split isolates record dependence but does not establish generalization across scenes, roles, or naturalistic dialogue. It is not a substitute for a broad speech-generation benchmark. Two target texts, one scene label, no public context-audio records, and deterministic source emotion-intensity mappings keep the present setting narrow even when learned planners improve on the admitted endpoints. Future extensions need license-cleared speech evidence, richer source-disjoint variation, and independent cue and plan annotations before new cases enter scored comparisons. Ethical risk remains concrete because speech systems may infer affect, intent, or stance from sensitive audio cues. VoxReason reduces this risk by requiring source links for every delivery field and excluding waveform-outcome and identity claims from the present evaluation. The absence of a listening panel is therefore a scope choice, not an omitted substitute for these endpoints: because the accepted claims are predicates over citations, typed delivery fields, and local cue edits, the current record-level evaluation does not require recruited evaluation participants. A listening study would answer downstream waveform questions and would need separate endpoints. 8A human-subject extension would require separate governance, preregistered endpoints, attention checks, and shared files that exclude raw private audio, credentials, checkpoints, and participant metadata. 

8 CONCLUSION 

VoxReason shifts evaluation upstream, before waveform rating, to the accountable decision that precedes it. If a system fails to show which record justified a delivery choice, a plausible-sounding output may still reflect the wrong reasoning process. By making the pre-synthesis plan explicit and checkable, VoxReason turns that hidden step into a measurable object. In the evaluated con-trolled source-label setting, cited source records change grounded planning more than parameter count does, while source-key and source-emotion controls show exactly where that claim stops. For dialogue agents, narration systems, and role-conditioned TTS, the immediate lesson is simple: be-fore asking whether a rendered voice sounds right, ask whether the underlying delivery choice was warranted by the record; only then does waveform evaluation have a grounded object to judge.
