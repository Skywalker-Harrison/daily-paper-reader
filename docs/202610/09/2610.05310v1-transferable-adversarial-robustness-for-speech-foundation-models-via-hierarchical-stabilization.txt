ABSTRACT 

Frozen speech foundation models (SFMs) make downstream adap-tation efficient: the backbone can stay fixed while a task learns layer fusion and a lightweight classifier. Full adversarial fine-tuning is a standard route to robustness, but generating adversarial examples and updating the backbone for every task sacrifices that efficiency. We ask whether robustness can instead be learned before future tasks are known. For a frozen backbone and linear classifier, robustness can be understood through the interaction between representation stability and decision-boundary margin. This leads directly to our design: we stabilize representations across the hidden layers, rather than only the final layer, while preserving clean representations; af-ter clean adaptation selects the layer mixture, we keep it fixed and enlarge only the classifier margin, without downstream adversarial examples. We evaluate Wav2Vec2, HuBERT, and WavLM Large on four tasks under adaptive 30 dB attacks. Across 12 backbone– task pairs, hierarchical robustification improves robust accuracy by 46.4 pp, while margin refinement adds 4.0 pp for 1.1 pp of clean accuracy. Code and configurations are available at github.com/ arefmousavi/hierarchical-robust-sfm .

Index Terms — speech foundation models, adversarial robust-ness, adversarial training, transfer learning, layer fusion 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Frozen speech foundation models (SFMs) support efficient down-stream adaptation because the backbone can remain fixed. In the SUPERB-style classification setting considered here, a task learns a weighted fusion of frozen layer representations together with a lightweight classifier [1]. Large-scale evaluation shows that learn-able layer fusion outperforms final-layer-only evaluation in most cases, while preferred depths vary across tasks [2, 3]. Full task-specific adversarial fine-tuning is a direct route to robustness, but iterative adversarial-example generation and end-to-end backbone updates are costly and must be repeated for every new task [4, 5]. We therefore ask whether the efficiency of frozen-backbone adap-tation can be retained while robustness is learned before future downstream tasks are known. This goal requires more than a robust terminal embedding. FARE in Robust CLIP stabilizes the CLIP vision-encoder output so existing downstream systems can reuse the robust encoder without retraining [6, 7]. In our setting, however, the future speech task chooses both a layer mixture and a classifier after backbone robus-tification. For a frozen backbone and linear classifier, robustness is governed by the interaction between representation stability and decision-boundary margin. Let zα(x) be the fused representation,

Before tasks are known, we stabilize representations across the hidden layers, rather than only the final layer, while preserving clean representations. Keeping the fusion fixed lets the margin objective change deci-sion geometry without changing the representation selected by clean adaptation, and requires no downstream adversarial examples. Fig. 1 summarizes the resulting pipeline and its movement–margin inter-pretation. Prior work establishes that robust pretrained representations can transfer robustness to downstream classifiers and studies how that ro-bustness behaves during adaptation [5, 8, 9, 10, 11]. In speech, Wu et al. study attacks when the downstream task, layer-fusion weights, and classifier are unknown to the attacker [12], Olivier et al. demon-strate adversarial transfer across self-supervised speech models [13], and Wang et al. study task-specific adversarial fine-tuning for robust ASR [14]. These works do not address the combination studied here: future task-specific layer fusion and classifier are unknown during robustification, representations must therefore be stabilized across layers, and the downstream decision geometry is refined after adap-tation, using no adversarial examples. Our main contributions are: (1) Hierarchy-aware adversarial robustification. We introduce an adversarial objective matched to frozen SFM layer fusion that stabilizes representations across hid-den layers while preserving clean representations. (2) Movement-margin formulation. We characterize robustness through the in-teraction between adversarial representation movement and distance to the nearest classifier boundary, and show that across-layer sta-bilization bounds this movement for every layer fusion a task may select. (3) Clean margin-aware classifier refinement. We refine only the downstream classifier, with the robust backbone and learned fusion fixed, using no downstream adversarial examples. We evalu-ate Wav2Vec2, HuBERT, and WavLM Large on four complementary speech classification tasks using adaptive white-box attacks.

3. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP Backbones and tasks. We evaluate Wav2Vec2 Large, HuBERT Large, and WavLM Large, which differ in pretraining objective and data [15, 16, 17]. We use Wav2Vec2 Large as the primary backbone for mechanism, attack, and sensitivity analyses, while all three back-bones assess transfer breadth. We use four utterance-level tasks un-der the same frozen-backbone, learned-fusion, linear-head interface, spanning complementary speech factors: Speech Commands key-word spotting (KS; content) [18], Fluent Speech Commands intent classification (IC; semantics) [19], VoxCeleb1 speaker identification (SID; speaker) [20], and four-class IEMOCAP emotion recognition (ER; paralinguistics) [21, 1]. Comparisons are within backbone, not across differently pretrained models [3].

Clean margin refinement. STAGE 2 adds RA on every pair, 4.0 pp on average for 1.1 pp CA, using no downstream adversarial examples; the soft surrogate beats the hard minimum by 2.5 pp RA with a larger m10 (Table 2(b)). Using downstream adversarial ex-amples, head training gains 2.7 pp more RA for 2.1 pp less CA; full AT reaches 63.0% mean RA against our 50.4% on the primary back-bone, but updates the backbone per task. 

Hierarchy and preservation. STAGE 1 exceeds mean all-layer stabilization by 4.5 pp RA (Table 2(a)), and Fig. 2(a) shows why: the terminal-layer objective attains the lowest drift at layer L but the highest drift at most earlier depths, while worst-layer weighting pushes S TAGE 1’s peak drift below the layer-mean variant. Remov-ing Lpres . gives the largest movement reduction (93.2%) yet costs 18.7 pp CA and 30.3 pp RA against S TAGE 1: reduced movement alone is not robustness. 

Adaptive evaluation. The normalized-margin attack is strongest on KS, IC, and ER and AutoPGD-CE on SID, and the union re-moves a further 1.0–2.8 pp, so the attacks fail on different examples (Table 2(c)). The method ranking is unchanged across 24–36 dB (Fig. 2(b)). 

Training cost. On an RTX 4090, S TAGE 1 costs 18.9 GPU-h once per backbone, while S TAGE 2 costs 0.37 GPU-h per task, compared with 10.7 GPU-h per task for full AT. 

5. CONCLUSION 

Stabilizing the hidden hierarchy before tasks are known transfers robustness beyond terminal-layer stabilization; clean margin refine-ment further improves robustness without downstream adversarial examples. Across 12 backbone–task pairs, the full method improves mean RA by 50.4 pp over Standard+CE at a 3.2 pp CA cost under the evaluated digital waveform attacks. Acknowledgment.
